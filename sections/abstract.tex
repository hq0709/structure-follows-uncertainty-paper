Cyclic structure helps learned estimation at the source of uncertainty.
An observed increment sets the rotation angle. An uncertain increment calls
for a belief update that mixes cyclic shifts and corrects with pointer readings.
In a four-position valve task, exact cyclic updates reach $1.000$ accuracy
from a terminal label or reward, compared with chance for standard recurrent
models and learned-angle rotation. At a $0.01$ token misread rate, modeling
misreads reduces terminal-label negative log likelihood from $5.839$ to $0.966$.
In valve control, phase rotation reaches $0.997$ success at four times the
longest training horizon, compared with $0.801$ for the best budget-matched
baseline. With a shared planner, the token-constrained filter improves utility
over the GRU in 47 of 75 settings by paired bootstrap, chiefly through sensing
decisions. On recorded heading data, gyroscope increments support $29.6^\circ$
error for phase rotation and $27.4^\circ$ for a GRU at 240~s after training on
10~s windows. Estimating bias during rest reduces rotation error at 120~s from
$20.2^\circ$ to $12.8^\circ$. On recorded gait data with unobserved increments,
learned-angle rotation stays at chance, and the cyclic filter cuts phase RMSE
by $27\%$ under 3~s dropouts, from $12.33\%$ for an LSTM to $9.04\%$.
