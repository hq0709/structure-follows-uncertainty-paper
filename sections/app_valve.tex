\section{Valve Control}
\label{app:valve}
The valve-control experiment in Section~\ref{sec:transfer} shows that phase
rotation learned from terminal reward succeeds beyond the training horizon.
The original-budget and fixed-rate comparisons test the same task across
tuning protocols.

\subsection{Symbolic valve task and provenance}
\label{app:robot}
In the $\Z_4$ operate task, the policy sees the initial pointer for two
steps, the target, and the command and execution cues for intervening
turns. It learns corrective turns and the finish action from terminal
reward. Training uses 1--8 turns. The wider evaluation
includes 32, 64, and 128 turns and changes the clockwise probability.
The programmed counter is an oracle. The learned cyclic counter estimates
observation-conditioned shifts of its belief and learns an action head.

The RLBench/CoppeliaSim visualization updates the valve pose with
\texttt{set\_orientation} and moves the arm with a scripted macro. Its checks
compare rendered poses with internal simulator state.

\subsection{Training and evaluation}
The policies learn from terminal reward on the operate task. The re-tuned
comparison triples the original tuning budget and widens the learning-rate
grid. Every family receives 12{,}000 A2C updates
and the learning-rate grid $\{3\times10^{-4},10^{-3},3\times10^{-3},10^{-2}\}$.
The GRU and LSTM also use widths 16, 32, and 64. Selection uses $N=8$ success
on tuning seeds 100 and 101. The selected learning rates are evaluated on five
fresh seeds (0--4), with 4{,}096 balanced greedy episodes per seed and horizon.
The programmed counter succeeds at every horizon. At $N=128$, sixteen
times the longest training horizon, phase rotation reaches $0.673$ and the
best learned baseline reaches $0.684$.
