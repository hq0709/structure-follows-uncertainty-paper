\section{Full Increment-Uncertainty Sweep}
\label{app:sweep}

Section~\ref{sec:sweep} shows how exact cyclic transitions support learning
from sparse signals. The four-position valve starts from an exact pointer
and moves by $-1$, $0$, or $+1$ with probabilities $(0.25,0.50,0.25)$.
With probability $r$, each delivered token is independently replaced with
one of the other two tokens, chosen uniformly. The increments regime supplies
all tokens and only the initial pointer. The missing regime erases four
consecutive tokens in every twelve steps and supplies a noisy pointer every
twelve steps. That pointer reports the true position with probability $0.8$
and draws a uniform token otherwise.

All learned models train at the test misread rate on lengths $6$, $10$, and
$14$, with 3{,}000 Adam updates, batch size 96, and gradient clipping at norm 1.
Each model uses the learning-rate grid $\{10^{-3},3\times 10^{-3},10^{-2}\}$
and two tuning seeds. Dense supervision uses state NLL at every prefix, and
terminal supervision uses it at the final step. Reward training uses
REINFORCE with a sampled final answer, reward $+1$ for a correct answer and
$-1$ otherwise, and a running-mean baseline. We select the learning rate
by validation NLL for supervision and validation accuracy for reward.

The GRU and LSTM use widths 16 and 13, with
1{,}844 and 1{,}812 parameters. The learned-angle and measured-angle rotations
use 1{,}088 and 1{,}248 parameters, respectively. The cyclic filters use
196--197 parameters. Matched Bayes uses the simulator probabilities directly.
The measured-angle rotation uses a learned angle on erased turns and a learned
input term on steps with an erasure or a pointer reading.

All three cyclic filters mix exact shifts and correct the belief with a
pointer likelihood. The token-constrained filter treats the reported shift
as exact on delivered steps, and the unconstrained filter learns shift
probabilities from the cue. The misread-aware filter combines a global
learned misread probability with a learned increment prior to infer shift
probabilities given a token. It uses the prior directly for an erased token.

\subsection{Increments regime}
Table~\ref{tab:sweep} compares the clean and $r=0.01$ results at $L=40$
across training signals.

Both gated models fit the clean count under dense supervision. With a
terminal label, their NLL and the learned-angle rotation's NLL are
$1.386$--$1.387$, close to chance NLL of $1.386$ nats. The misread-aware
filter matches Bayes accuracy
at every tested rate under all three signals and reaches $0.972$ NLL at
$r=0.01$ with terminal reward (Figure~\ref{fig:sweep}b).

The misread-aware filter tracks matched Bayes at $L=40$ under dense
supervision (Table~\ref{tab:sweep-inc-dense}).

\subsection{Missing regime}
Modeling misreads and correcting with pointer readings brings the
filter's NLL close to matched Bayes under dense supervision
(Table~\ref{tab:sweep-missing-dense}).

At $r=0.3$ and $L=160$, both the misread-aware filter and matched Bayes
reach $1.263$ NLL with dense and terminal labels. The token-constrained filter
gives $1.341$ and $1.333$, respectively. Under terminal reward, the
misread-aware filter reaches $1.637$ NLL and $0.375$ accuracy. The GRU
gives $5.081$ NLL and $0.356$ accuracy, and the LSTM gives $3.988$ NLL and
$0.344$ accuracy.
